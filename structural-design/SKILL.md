## Establish the required result

Interpret the request in its available context. Distinguish the result sought,
the work authorized, and the choices the request fixes or leaves open. Support
each consequential interpretation through the request and applicable rules.
Use established dependencies to identify constraints and missing prerequisites
within the authorized work. Determine separately what the result must accomplish
and how it is to be presented.

For an example or comparison, identify the relationship it illustrates or the
claim it challenges and the scope the request assigns to it. Establish which
features affect the required result. For a proposed term, method, or arrangement,
determine whether the request adopts it, requires its examination, or permits
alternatives.

Compare readings that would change the result or authorized work against the
available context. Investigate a missing fact; make a delegated choice within
its stated constraints; obtain clarification for a remaining difference that
changes what is required or permitted. Continue work whose validity is
independent of that unresolved difference.

Derive the behavior, information, or understanding the result must supply from
the request, supporting facts, and applicable rules. Use that basis to determine
which instructions apply.

For each requirement, identify the information needed to assess satisfaction
and select a method that can establish the required property. Identify
preservation obligations. Use the same requirements to assess an existing
arrangement and a replacement.

Identify missing facts that could change a responsibility, grouping, or
dependency decision. Obtain those facts through authorized means or identify
the decision they prevent. Keep an unresolved assumption distinguishable from
a supported basis for selecting an arrangement.

## Identify contributions and dependencies

Establish which contributions the arrangement must produce or maintain and
which it only consumes from existing or external providers. Justify a new
production or maintenance responsibility through the authorized task.

Distinguish work needed to produce or assess the current result from
responsibilities that must continue afterward. Justify each continuing
responsibility through a later use or preservation obligation within the
authorized task.

For each proposed part, identify the behavior it performs, the information it
establishes or preserves, or the understanding it enables. Identify what it
requires from other parts to supply that result. Derive these contributions
from the required result independently of proposed names and locations.

When constructing or revising an arrangement, work from required contributions
to available ways of supplying them and the conditions under which those ways
work. Establish those relationships to the detail needed to justify the
arrangement. Use the conditions to constrain choices that must work together,
distinguishing what the arrangement establishes from what it requires of
external suppliers. Obtain a missing construction method where it is needed
to establish the arrangement, or identify the capability that remains unresolved.

Use the relevant language, tool, or discipline's established meanings for its
constructs. State a local meaning where the task requires a different use.
Give a proposed name the meaning established by its content or responsibility.

For each dependency, identify the receiving part, supplying part, and required
information or behavior. Compare what the receiver requires with what the
supplier establishes under the conditions of use. Obtain or establish a missing
property needed for that relationship, or keep the dependency unresolved.

Where a required property can cease to hold before or during a dependent use,
establish when and for how long it must hold. Identify what preserves that
condition or prevents the dependent use when it does not hold.

Identify the parts that make or maintain a choice, the information they need,
and what other parts require from the result. Determine which decisions can be
made within each part and which require information from elsewhere.

For proposed shared behavior or instructions, identify the required behavior,
information, and assumptions that remain the same across their uses. Determine
which differences are inputs to the same procedure and which require a different
procedure or prerequisites. Establish the applicability of the shared behavior
from those facts.

## Compare and select an arrangement

Use the identified contributions and dependencies to compare grouping,
separation, and placement. For each proposed separation, identify what it changes
about access, understanding, execution, maintenance, or other required behavior.
Determine what the actual mechanisms control and what depends on stated rules.

Compare the dependencies each alternative adds, removes, or retains. Determine
which information its parts must share or keep consistent, which uses remain
possible, and which decisions can change independently. Justify placement
through those relationships and consequences.

Trace the changes the task or available evidence gives reason to expect.
Determine which parts each change would affect and why. Distinguish a change in
what is supplied from a change in how it is produced. Use those consequences
to assess the dependencies created by each arrangement.

Derive required execution or explanation order from what must be available for
an action or understanding to occur. Establish the dependency requiring that
order independently of the proposed grouping.

Select among alternatives using the task's priorities and constraints. State
a tradeoff through the differing properties and their consequences. Preserve
open alternatives where the available basis permits several choices. Investigate
further to resolve a specified difference that could change the selection,
in proportion to the consequences of a mistaken choice.

Produce the arrangement or assessment requested by the task. Preserve fixed
choices and establish the relationships selected through the comparison.

## Examine the arrangement

For each applicable requirement, follow the required behavior, information,
or understanding through the parts that supply it. Check that information is
available where it is used, that suppliers establish what receivers require,
and that their combined result satisfies the requirement.

Examine each boundary through the effects used to select it. Trace the effects
of relevant changes on what must remain usable or unchanged. Use the actual
access and execution mechanisms to establish enforced properties. Distinguish
predicted effects from effects observed in use.

Identify the affected part, required property, examination method, supporting
evidence, and finding. Establish required execution from observations of the
performed actions. Compare the task requirements and applicable procedural
actions with the completed examinations. If that comparison establishes an
omitted check, unsupported finding, or discrepancy, identify the requirement
and information that expose it.

## Correct discrepancies and determine completion

For an established discrepancy, locate the responsible assumption,
responsibility, grouping, or dependency. Return to that decision and select
a correction through its effects on the required result. Compare retaining,
replacing, dividing, combining, reordering, or removing material where those
choices address the discrepancy.

Apply changes within the task's authority. In an assessment-only task, present
the supported correction as a proposal. Re-examine the changed result and the
behavior or dependencies affected by the change. A changed requirement or
supporting fact also requires reconsidering the decisions and examinations
that depend on it. Continue correction where the new examination establishes
a remaining discrepancy.

Determine whether each unresolved point is a permitted uncertainty or choice,
or leaves required work unfinished. Obtain the information or authorization
needed for unfinished work where the task permits. Otherwise identify what
remains required and what prevents establishing it.

Deliver the requested result as complete when examination of that result
establishes satisfaction of each applicable requirement and required procedural
action. Base the completion judgment on those findings and their evidence.
