## Establish the intended meaning

Determine the requested text, its intended use, and the information available
to its readers. Establish the understanding or action the text must support
for those readers. Select each assertion, instruction, and supporting
explanation through its contribution to that use and its basis in the request
and supporting material. Establish the distinctions and relationships those
readers must recover.
Distinguish fixed wording and identifiers from expressions open to revision.
Determine which changes to meaning the task authorizes.

Identify which supplied expressions are proposed names, examples, quotations,
or adopted wording through their role in the request. Select their use in the
result through the meaning its readers must recover. Establish the status of
facts, estimates, proposals, permissions, obligations, and unknowns before
expressing them.

## Construct sentences

For each statement, identify who or what it concerns, the action, state, or
relationship expressed, and the conditions governing it. Determine which
participants are known and which remain unidentified. Choose grammatical
subjects and verbs that express those relationships with their established
scope and attribution.

Use verbs whose participants and asserted effects are recoverable. Expand
a process noun where the noun leaves a required action or relationship
unexpressed. Select active or passive construction through the information
established and the subject the reader needs to follow. Preserve an unknown
participant as unknown.

Attach each condition, exception, qualification, and time expression to the
action, participant, or assertion it governs. Determine how grammatical
attachment and punctuation affect that scope. For a governing condition,
determine what is asserted, permitted, or required when it holds and when it
does not. Express those relationships through the conditional and modal wording.
Separate or restate expressions where their combination would apply a
qualification to a different assertion.

Determine the set or quantity governed by a quantifier and the assertion
governed by negation. Express permission, obligation, possibility, and certainty
according to the intended meaning. State required actions and relationships
directly. Retain exclusions and negative facts that the meaning requires.

Identify the referent of each pronoun, demonstrative, abbreviation, and
comparative expression through the reader's available context. Supply a name,
description, or comparison basis where that context permits readings with
different required consequences. Use a term consistently within its stated
meaning and scope.

Use the grammar and established conventions of the language being composed.
Compare sentence constructions through their effects on interpretation.
Reposition an interruption, divide a sentence, or combine related statements
where the change makes the governing relationship recoverable while preserving
its conditions and qualifications.

## Connect statements

Identify what the wording between statements lets the reader infer about their
relationship. Select conjunctions, transitions, and punctuation whose meanings
match the relationship established by the supporting content. Preserve the
difference between a sequence of events, a cause, a condition, a comparison,
and separately asserted facts wherever it affects the required meaning.

Make the information needed to interpret a statement available where it is
used. Arrange statements through the reader's required questions and the
understanding each statement presupposes. Use the language's information order
to establish the subject being continued and the point being developed.

Keep a statement's scope and referents recoverable across sentence and
paragraph boundaries. When combining statements, determine whether shared
wording changes which participants, conditions, or qualifications apply.
When separating statements, retain the connection needed to interpret them.

Produce the requested composition, revision, or assessment through those
choices. Preserve exact wording where the task fixes it and the intended
meaning where the task permits reformulation.

## Examine and revise the text

Recover the assertions and directions from the current text using the
information available to its intended readers. Resolve references, grammatical
attachments, conditions, quantifiers, negation, attribution, and statement
connections through the actual language conventions. Compare the recovered
meaning with the meaning established from the task and supporting material.

Identify competing readings that change a required conclusion or action.
Examine cases that distinguish those readings and compare their consequences
with the requested meaning. Check added assertions and removed qualifications
against the changes the task authorizes.

For an established discrepancy, locate the responsible wording, attachment,
reference, or connection. Apply revisions within the task's authority and
re-examine the affected statements and their dependent uses. In an assessment-only
task, present the supported finding and any proposed correction. Examine proposed
wording before presenting it as a correction. Obtain missing facts or decisions
within the task's authority where their absence prevents establishing a required
meaning. Preserve uncertainty or permitted choices where those are part of the
requested result.

Compare examination coverage with the requested work, required meaning, and
preservation obligations. Deliver composed or revised text as complete when
examination of its current wording establishes that readers can recover the
required meaning under the stated conditions of use. Establish completion of
an assessment through findings answering the requested questions about the
examined wording. For required work left unfinished, identify what remains
unestablished and the information, decision, authority, or capability needed
to proceed.
